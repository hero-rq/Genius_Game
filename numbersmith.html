The Four Rules

1

You are given a base digit, a fixed count of how many times it must be used, and a target number to reach.

2

You must build a formula that equals the target using the base digit exactly that many times — no more, no less.

3

Any mathematical operation is allowed — addition, subtraction, multiplication, division, roots, factorials, exponents, derivatives, concatenation, and so on.

4

Optionally, a "varies" value is given — meaning you must find that many distinct formulas for the same target.

On counting: the counter below your workings tallies how many times your chosen digit appears as a literal. Operators, parentheses, and symbols like √ or ! don't cost a digit. It's a guide, not a judge — you and your evaluator decide what truly counts.
